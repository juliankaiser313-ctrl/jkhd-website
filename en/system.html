// Structure

This is what a system of ours looks like.

Six layers, from the data connection through to monitored operation. For each layer this page says what goes in — and which mistake we rule out there before it costs money.

// The layers

Every layer has a job — and an enemy.

A system rarely fails because of the idea. It fails at the point where someone left one of these layers out.

01

Data & connectivity

Raw data arrives tick by tick and is aggregated into bars that we produce ourselves — not bought in and not smoothed by the vendor.

Built in
  • Connection to the data feed, with resumption after a drop-out
  • Our own bar aggregation from ticks
  • Gap and outlier checks, with a log
  • Time zones, trading hours and contract rolls handled properly
  • Immutable storage of the raw data
Watched for
  • Look-ahead: no value may be available earlier than it actually was
  • Silent gaps: missing bars are not quietly filled in, they are flagged
  • Roll dates: a contract change is not a price jump
  • Subsequent corrections from the vendor are versioned, not overwritten
02

Research & signal discovery

We do not look for one strategy; we map an entire signal family systematically across thousands of parameter combinations.

Built in
  • Signal families instead of individual cases
  • Complete parameter mapping, every cell evaluated separately
  • Separate periods for search and confirmation
  • A baseline for comparison — a signal must beat doing nothing
  • Every run reproducible: seed, data vintage, parameters
Watched for
  • Overfitting: the more combinations, the more surely you find coincidence
  • Selection blindness: showing only the winners shows nothing
  • Regime change: what carried one year need not carry the next
  • Data reuse: the same period loses meaning with every test
03

Statistical hardening tests

The stage where the majority of all candidates die. A result must show that it could not equally well have arisen by pure chance.

Built in
  • Multiple-testing correction across the entire search
  • White’s Reality Check against the chance distribution
  • Out-of-sample confirmation on untouched data
  • Fidelity report: deviation between test and real behaviour
  • Results documented and archived, rejections included
Watched for
  • The best of a thousand is impressive by construction — even if all of them are noise
  • Pressure for results: a passed test is not repeated until it fits
  • Too short a history: too little data delivers any answer you want
  • Costs and slippage belong in the test, not in the footnote
04

Risk & capital

The limits are fixed before the first trade and are kept separately for each account. They are part of the system, not an afterthought bolted on.

Built in
  • Daily loss limit per account
  • Trailing drawdown limit, carried forward continuously
  • Mark-to-market breaker that stops immediately — not only at the close
  • Position sizes from rules, not from confidence
  • A clearly defined state after the stop: what happens to open positions
Watched for
  • Concentration risk: several signals that in truth bet on the same thing
  • The one bad day determines the design, not the average
  • Limits without enforcement are declarations of intent — here code enforces them
  • Manual intervention is not risk management
05

Execution & orders

Between signal and market lies the place where most of the return is lost — quietly and without an error message.

Built in
  • Order management with defined behaviour on partial fills
  • Bracket logic fixed at signal time, not after the fact
  • Reconciliation between expected and actual execution
  • Restart after a connection loss without duplicate orders
  • Complete order log, immutable
Watched for
  • Slippage and fees decide whether an idea makes or loses money
  • Latency: a signal that arrives too late is a different signal
  • Duplicate orders after a restart are the most expensive software bug there is
  • Orphaned positions without their matching protection
06

Operation & monitoring

A system nobody watches is a system whose failure you hear about last. Watchdogs monitor the bot, and a guard monitors the watchdogs.

Built in
  • Watchdog chain with self-healing and an alerting path
  • Shadow tracking: computed against actual result
  • Drift detection — if a signal loses its effect, it is retired
  • Complete logs, every state versioned
  • Defined emergency operation and documented restart
Watched for
  • Silent failure: the system appears to run but no longer trades
  • Alert fatigue: whoever reports everything reports nothing
  • Dependencies: feed, broker, clock, operating system — any of them can fail
  • Traceability: months later, every trade must still be explainable

// Honestly

What such a system does not do.

No prediction

A system does not predict the market. It applies a rule with discipline and limits the damage when the rule is not working.

No permanence

Signals lose their effect. That is why drift detection is built in and not optional — retiring a signal is part of operating one.

No freedom from losses

Losing days are planned for, not a surprise. The question is never whether they come, but how deep they are allowed to go.

Not self-running

Operation, monitoring and upkeep are work. Anyone who does not plan for them is not running a system but a risk.
