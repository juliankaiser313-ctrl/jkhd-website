How a system tries to make money.

A trading program always carries an assumption about why a price moves. This page shows the eight basic kinds — in plain language and with the catch each one has.

// The basic kinds

Eight ways a program tries to make money

Every automated trading system belongs to one of these families, or mixes several. Here is what each one does and where it fails. Not a manual — just the idea behind it.

01

Arbitrage

The same thing at two prices. Whoever sees both at once buys cheaply there and sells dearly here — in the same moment, without betting on a direction.

The catch

The gap is tiny and gone in milliseconds. Without the shortest line and a machine next to the exchange you arrive too late.

02

Market making

Always quote a price to buy and a price to sell, and earn the spread between them. The money comes from turnover, not from direction.

The catch

You get filled in full exactly when everyone runs the same way — and you are left holding the position nobody wants.

03

Trend

What moves often keeps moving. You join the direction that is already running and stay until it breaks.

The catch

Many small false starts stand against a few large hits. Whoever cannot sit through the false starts stops before the hit.

04

Mean reversion

A spike usually comes back. You trade against the exaggeration and wait for the price to settle.

The catch

Sometimes the spike is not a spike but a beginning. Then the loss grows with every step you add.

05

Pairs trading

Two things that normally move together drift apart for a while: sell the dearer one, buy the cheaper one, wait for them to meet again.

The catch

“Normally” is an assumption about the past. If a company really changes, the pair never comes back together.

06

Carry

Some positions pay you for holding them — an interest difference, a roll gain, an insurance premium. The money comes from waiting, not moving.

The catch

It goes well for a long time and then very badly once. The premium is the payment for exactly that rare once.

07

Events

Figures, decisions, dates: the price jumps when something new becomes known. What is traded is the reaction, not your own opinion.

The catch

The reaction takes milliseconds, and part of the news is already in the price. Whoever reads it first trades the second wave.

08

Order book

The order book shows who wants how much at which price. The jostling of orders tells you something about the next small step.

The catch

That holds for seconds, not days. And orders lie: they are placed to be seen and then withdrawn.
